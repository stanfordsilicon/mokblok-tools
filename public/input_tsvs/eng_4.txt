Linguists predict that 50% or more of the world’s languages may become extinct this century. Meanwhile, out of the approximately 7,000 living human languages, all but 100–or roughly 98%–are categorized as “Digitally Disadvantaged Languages” (DDLs) by the Unicode Consortium, the non-profit agency committed to ensuring that all human writing systems can be represented digitally across technical platforms. 
SILICON, the Stanford Initiative on Language Inclusion and Conservation in Old and New Media, aims to support Digitally Disadvantaged Languages across all stages of the digital inclusion journey: encoding, typeface design, keyboard design, OCR, Machine Translation, AI development, and more. 

In order to advance digital equity for all living and historic scripts, SILICON invites applications for financial support designed to launch or complete their projects.

Do you speak a Digitally Disadvantaged Language? 

Not all languages work the same on your phone or computer. There exists significant disparity in the availability of keyboards, AI, fonts, text-to-speech, spell check, and a host of other technologies. 

How can you play a role in bridging the digital divide? An hour or two or your time could make the world of difference for a Digitally Disadvantaged Language. Fill out this short Google form to get involved with the SILICON community.


The mission of SILICON is to advance digital inclusion for the world’s most linguistically underserved populations by solving the practical, real-world problems that hold 6000+ languages back in the 21st century all across the Americas, Asia, Africa, and beyond. From missing fonts and keyboards, to spell checkers and machine translation, SILICON works across sectors to patch the digital tools that most people take for granted—but which leave billions of others locked out.
